Quarterly Deep Cleaning vs. Regular Service

Regular service and periodic deep cleaning serve different purposes — most facilities need both.

Some facility managers assume that if regular janitorial service is happening consistently, deep cleaning is optional. In practice, the two serve different purposes, and most commercial facilities need both.

What Regular Service Actually Maintains

Nightly or scheduled janitorial service maintains a baseline — trash, restrooms, surface dust, daily floor upkeep. It is designed to keep a facility presentable and hygienic day to day, not to reverse accumulated wear.

What Deep Cleaning Addresses

Deep cleaning — carpet extraction, floor stripping and waxing, high dusting of vents and shelving, grout and tile restoration — addresses buildup that accumulates gradually over months, not days. No amount of nightly vacuuming replaces a periodic carpet extraction, and no daily mop replaces a floor strip-and-wax cycle.

Why Skipping Deep Cleaning Costs More Later

Deferred floor and carpet maintenance accelerates wear on flooring that is expensive to replace. A quarterly or semi-annual deep-clean add-on typically costs far less than early flooring replacement caused by years of deferred maintenance.

How to Schedule It

Deep cleaning is usually added onto an existing nightly or day-porter contract on a quarterly or semi-annual cadence, scheduled during off-hours to avoid disrupting operations. Ask us to include a deep-clean cadence in your written scope if it is not already part of your current contract.
