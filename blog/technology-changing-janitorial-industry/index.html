How Technology Is Changing Janitorial Service

From smarter restroom monitoring to digital task verification, the industry looks different than it did five years ago.

Janitorial service may not seem like an industry at the frontier of technological change, but the day-to-day delivery of the work looks meaningfully different than it did five years ago.

Digital Cleaning Logs

Paper cleaning logs have largely given way to digital platforms. Cleaning staff check in and log completed tasks via mobile apps, giving facility managers a real-time record of what was actually done — a significant upgrade from taking a crew's word for it.

Restroom Usage Monitoring

Occupancy sensors and supply-level monitoring allow cleaning teams to respond to actual usage rather than a fixed schedule alone — a heavily used restroom gets checked more often, one that sees little traffic does not get an unnecessary visit.

Better Floor Care Equipment

Modern floor-care equipment covers more ground per hour with more consistent results than older equipment, which matters directly for large industrial and distribution floors common across Goodyear's warehouse corridor.

Route and Scheduling Optimization

Better scheduling tools help providers route crews more efficiently across multiple facilities, which shows up as more reliable arrival times and coverage for clients.

What to Ask Your Provider

When evaluating a janitorial company, ask whether they use digital task verification and what equipment they bring for larger floor areas — the answers say a lot about how seriously they take consistency.
