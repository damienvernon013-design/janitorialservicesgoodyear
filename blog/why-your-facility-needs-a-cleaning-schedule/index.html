Why Your Facility Needs a Written Cleaning Schedule

Ad-hoc cleaning — clean it when it looks dirty — is one of the more expensive habits a facility manager can fall into.

A documented cleaning schedule — not a reactive "clean it when it looks dirty" approach — is what separates a facility that stays consistently presentable from one that cycles between acceptable and embarrassing.

What a Cleaning Schedule Actually Is

A written schedule specifies which tasks happen, how often, and in which areas — daily, weekly and monthly or quarterly — calibrated to the realistic dirt accumulation and traffic in your specific space.

Why Consistency Beats Intensity

Businesses sometimes try to make up for inconsistent cleaning with an occasional deep clean. Regular, moderate cleaning generally controls dust, dirt and surface buildup more effectively than infrequent intensive cleans — the same logic as daily brushing beating a once-a-month scrub.

A Typical Breakdown by Frequency

Daily: trash and recycling removal, restroom sanitation and restocking, break room wipe-down, vacuuming of high-traffic carpet, spot-cleaning of glass and high-touch surfaces.

Weekly: full vacuuming of all carpeted areas, mopping of hard floors, dusting of desks and shelving, interior glass and partition cleaning.

Monthly/Quarterly: deep restroom scrub, floor buffing or waxing, vent cleaning, carpet extraction, exterior window washing.

Building Yours

A written scope of work becomes your schedule and your accountability tool at the same time — you know exactly what should happen and when, and you have something concrete to hold a crew to.
